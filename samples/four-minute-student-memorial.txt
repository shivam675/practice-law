IN THE SUPREME COURT OF DEVAGARH

ANANYA IYER v. STATE OF DEVAGARH

MEMORIAL FOR THE APPLICANT

Issue

Whether repeated suspension of mobile internet services without a fresh,
reasoned review violates the applicant's rights to speech, occupation, and
effective judicial review.

Submission

The applicant submits that each suspension order must independently satisfy
legality, necessity, and proportionality. The record shows that four materially
identical orders were issued. None identifies new evidence, explains why a less
restrictive measure was unavailable, or addresses the continuing harm to
students, advocates, journalists, and small businesses.

First, repetition is not review. A lawful review must examine current facts and
record reasons capable of being tested. Copying the conclusion from an earlier
order converts a temporary emergency measure into an indefinite restriction.

Second, the orders are not shown to be necessary. The State identifies a
legitimate public-safety concern, but it does not connect that concern to the
geographic scope, duration, or services blocked. Targeted restrictions,
enforcement against identified actors, and narrower service controls were not
meaningfully considered.

Third, the balance of harm favours the applicant. The restriction prevents
ordinary communication and access to work, education, legal assistance, and
public information. Those concrete harms grow each day, while the State's
justification remains general.

Relief requested

The applicant asks the Court to set aside the latest suspension order and
require any future order to state current evidence, consider less restrictive
alternatives, define a narrow scope and duration, and receive prompt independent
review.

Oral outline

1. Every renewal requires fresh reasons.
2. The State has not proved that a region-wide shutdown is necessary.
3. Less restrictive alternatives were not considered.
4. The remedy sought preserves emergency powers while making them reviewable.
