The text of Order XLI Rule 10 CPC must be read as a whole. Its main provision commences with the discretionary word “[t]he Appellate Court may… demand… security for the costs”. The proviso appended thereto utilises mandatory language (“shall”) but only operates in relation to a defined class of cases: where the appellant resides outside India and “is not possessed of any sufficient immovable property within India other than the property (if any) to which the appeal relates”. The expression “property… to which the appeal relates” is qualified by its immediate context — namely, “immovable property”. Thus, the proviso presupposes proceedings concerning immovable property; it cannot be severed from that requirement to create a free-standing mandatory rule based exclusively upon foreign residence.

This interpretation is confirmed by this Hon’ble Court’s Division Bench in Communication Components Antenna Inc. v ACE Technologies Corp [2025] (DHC, DJB), which held that the proviso to Order XXV Rule 1 CPC — whose language parallels that of Order XLI Rule 10 — applies only to suits concerning immovable property. To the extent that earlier Single Judge judgments, such as Alpha Oil International v. M.T. Chem Lily [BHC, SJB], had expressed a contrary view or failed to engage fully with the proviso’s territorial limitation, the Division Bench has now settled the question: “The Proviso to Rule 1(1) of Order XXV of the CPC is applicable only in respect to the suits relating to immovable property.” This ratio governs the present application with equal force.
